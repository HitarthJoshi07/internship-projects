Fourier Transform

1  Introduction

The Fourier Transform is a mathematical technique used to represent a function in terms of its frequency components.

2  Fourier Transform

The continuous Fourier Transform of a function f(t)f(t)f(t) is defined as

\[ F(\omega) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t)\, e^{-i\omega t}\, dt \] where
  • f(t)f(t)f(t) is the time-domain signal,
  • F(ω)F(\omega)F(ω) is the frequency-domain representation,
  • ω\omegaω is the angular frequency,
  • i=−1i = \sqrt{-1}i=−1​.

3  Inverse Fourier Transform

The inverse Fourier Transform is given by

\[ f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\omega)\, e^{i\omega t}\, d\omega \]

5  Example

Consider the function

\[ f(t) = e^{-at^2}, \qquad a > 0. \] Its Fourier Transform is \[ F(\omega) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}\; e^{-\frac{\omega^2}{4a}}. \]

6  Conclusion

The Fourier Transform converts a signal from the time domain to the frequency domain, while the inverse Fourier Transform converts it back.
